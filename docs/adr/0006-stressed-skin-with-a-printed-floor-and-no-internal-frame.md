# The shell is a stressed skin with a printed floor, and it has no internal frame

The printed shell carries the loads. It has a printed floor, flanges on the inside of every bonded seam that act as ring frames, ribs on the large flat walls, and three carriers for the hardware: the monitor cradle between the rails, the BC-250 carrier, and the control panel frame. There is no frame of aluminium or plywood inside.

We examined a frame of 2020 aluminium extrusion with a skin that carries no load (option C of the study of 2026-11-11, `cad/candybar/shell/split.py` and `structure.py`). It fits only as one inclined rail on each side, about 1 mm from the hub and the monitor room, and no rear posts fit. The highest stress in the hand estimates for the skin is 0.41 MPa. The owner fills and paints the shell and does not take it apart, so panels that come off have no value here.

The owner chose on 2026-11-11:

- the seams of option B with the hood of option A;
- bonded seams whose pieces lock with keys and are then fastened for good;
- the present width (494.7), with a bezel plate that has no ledge at its sides, which gives the monitor 7.3 mm each side on its way out to the front;
- a hood with keys, which can be bolted or bonded.

## Consequences

Each seam is a joint and a rib. A joint must be rigid, because filler and paint crack over a joint that moves. The pieces must locate each other, so that a dry assembly with screws only holds its shape. A coupon proves the joint, its clearance and the adhesive before the parts are printed. The material is open: PETG on the A1, or ABS on an enclosed printer. The split and the joints must work for both.
